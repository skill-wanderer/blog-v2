The Sequel to The Tanegashima Shift

The Tanegashima essay was about the practitioner — what AI does to the developer's craft and identity.

This piece is about the market — what AI does to the price, accessibility,
and social meaning of the things developers build.

AI does not remove the need for engineering judgment.
It changes who can build, how fast, and what becomes economically possible.

⚖️ What AI Changes — and What It Does Not

⚡ What It Changes

  • ⏱️ Production time on simple components drops sharply
  • 📝 First drafts become cheap to throw away and redo
  • 👤 Small operators can take on team-shaped work
  • 💰 Baseline cost of a basic site falls meaningfully
⟷

🛡️ What It Does Not

  • 🧭 What to build and why still need a human
  • 🏛️ Architecture that survives the second feature
  • 🚀 Deployment, domains, email, analytics
  • 🔧 Maintenance after launch — patches, content, browser quirks

The Distinction That Matters

The craft is not gone.
The production cost of the baseline has dropped.

Conflating those two is how both hype and despair get manufactured.

The Old Model Was Not Wrong

Hiring a freelancer or agency for a website used to cost a meaningful sum
because the production cost was a meaningful sum.

Design, frontend, backend, hosting, deployment, security, accessibility, SEO, maintenance —
all of it was labor-intensive and earned through years of practice. That was a fair trade.

The old model was not wrong. It was shaped by the cost structure of its time.

🌾 History Rhymes

White rice did not destroy rice farming. It changed who got to eat well on a Tuesday.

Printing did not destroy authorship. It changed who got to read.

Photography did not destroy painting. It changed who got to keep a picture of their grandmother.

Desktop publishing did not destroy magazines. It changed who got to start one.

AI-assisted development will not destroy software engineering.
It will change who gets to have a digital presence worth pointing to. 🌱
