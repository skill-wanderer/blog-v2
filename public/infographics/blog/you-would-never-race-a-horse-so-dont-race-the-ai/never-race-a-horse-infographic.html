Humans have never won a fair fight with their bare hands, and we have never needed to. The winner is never the human on foot, and never the machine on its own. It is the human driving it.

😰 The Race My Learners Are Afraid of Losing

Now, what if I told you that this pattern is just as true in the age of AI? So many of the learners I mentor worry that AI is going to replace them. The words change from person to person, but the worry underneath is always the same.

"AI can already write this faster than I can."
"Why would anyone hire me?"
"What is the point of learning to code now?"

The worry is not wrong. The race is.

The worry is real and understandable, and I take it seriously every time I hear it. But race AI at the things AI is already good at, and yes, it is better than you, just as the horse is faster. No amount of training will change that.

🔧 Do Not Race the Horse. Build the Car.

So how do you beat a horse? The same way our ancestors did. They did not win by training harder to run.

Step 1 🙅

Skip the bare hands

Do not take the horse on at the one thing it was built for.

➤
Step 2 🔧

Compete where it cannot

A horse is fast and strong, but it will never build a car.

➤
Step 3 🏁

Bring the advantage home

Come back to the very same race, with a car, and win it easily.

🐎Then

Do not race the horse. Build the car.

🤖Now

Do not try to beat AI at what it is already good at. Compete on what it is bad at, and then bring that advantage home.

The lesson carries over almost word for word. Getting good at what AI cannot do is not about avoiding the work AI does. It is about coming back to that work with an advantage the AI does not have.

🏡 Better Than My Father, Just Not at His Game

Long before AI came along, I had to learn this lesson the hard way, in my own family. It started with a Vietnamese saying.

Con hơn cha là nhà có phúc.

"When a child grows up to be better than their father, the family is truly blessed."

A Vietnamese saying

Not a rivalry

For Vietnamese parents, a child who goes further than they did is a source of pride. Naturally, I took it seriously, almost as a duty. My first attempt at it did not end well.

🛢️ His race

Import and export
  1. 👣Following in his footsteps. My father was a well-known name in import and export, especially in oil and gas. Like father, like son.
  2. 💪Trying my very best. But the more I tried, the clearer it became that the goal was nowhere in sight.
  3. 🧱A race that would cost far too much to win. The skills it demands did not suit my personality. Winning would have meant turning myself into a different person.

💻 My race

Tech
  1. 🌧️Depressed for a while. At the time, admitting it simply felt like failure.
  2. 💡Finding my ground. People who pick things up quickly and are always hungry for more can be surprisingly resourceful in tech.
  3. 🏙️A small name in Hanoi's tech scene. Nothing grand, but a few people admire my work. In tech, I can finally say I have become better than my father.

Looking back, it was the same difference between "cannot" and "chose not to" that I wrote about in my last post.

🔑 Cannot, or chose not to?

Being better than someone does not mean beating them by the book, on the ground where they are strong and you are weak. It means finding the ground where you are strong, and fighting there instead.

"All is fair in love and war."

There is no rule anywhere that says I had to beat my father at import and export. To be fair, tech is a contest he never entered. But that is exactly the point.

From my own ground, I could see changes coming that my father, standing on his, did not
⚡

Electric vehicles would one day be more popular than petrol and diesel cars.

🤖

AI would open new opportunities while closing old ones, the same way the internet and machines did before it.

I did not beat my father at his own race. I stopped running it, and found one I could actually win.

The good news: with AI, you do not have to change careers the way I did to find your own ground. It is right there in the same field, in the parts of the work that AI is still bad at.

🧭 What AI Is Still Bad At: Ideas and Control

From what I see in my own work every day, there are two big things. Together, they are the ground you can win on without leaving your field.

🗺️
Ideas

Choosing the destination

  • It still cannot decide what is worth doing, and it does not set its own tasks.
  • It does not wake up one morning knowing which app the world needs, who it is for, or why anyone would pay for it.
  • It will happily build what you ask, but almost never stops, unprompted, to tell you that you are building the wrong thing.

📌 Remember the self-driving car: even when it does all of the driving, somebody still has to tell it where to go.

🛣️
Control

Keeping it on the road

  • It makes mistakes with total confidence.
  • It drifts away from what you asked for.
  • It declares the job finished long before it is.
  • The better models fail plausibly: what they hand you looks completely right and is quietly wrong.

The quality has improved enormously, and I mean that. But left on its own, it is still pretty lousy.

A good driver owns the drive

🚦 Learn to Drive, Not to Run

If you want to beat the horse, the skill you need is not running. It is driving. They are simply different skills, and getting faster at one does very little for the other.

🏃

Running

Plenty of people still run today, but for their health, not to get to work. Very few people plan their commute around their 10K time.

≠
🚗

Driving

The skill that actually beats the horse. No amount of sprint training will help you parallel park.

🔁 Writing a loop by hand is still good exercise

The skills AI already handles well are heading the same way running did.

🧠 It keeps your mind fit 🔍 It helps you understand what the AI hands back 🎛️ You need that when it is your turn to take control
Just do not make it the race you are training to win

Train for the skills that make you the driver

Endorsements

  2. D2

Getting around town

The back seat

I cannot drive a car. I chose not to learn, since a taxi gets me anywhere I need to go. That works perfectly well in town, but nobody has ever won a race from the back seat.

💺
Working with AI

The driver's seat

With AI, I made the opposite choice. Most of my working day is spent here: deciding what to build, and directing the AI that builds it.

Leave the running to the horse,
and learn to drive.

AI does make some old skills obsolete, or at least changes what they are for, much as the car did with running. The skills worth training for now are the ones that put you behind the wheel. So tell me: which race have you been training for? And is it one you can actually win?
