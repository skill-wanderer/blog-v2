"I Was Not Born for This"
Is True Only as Long as You Believe It

For years I believed I was not born to lead and not born to code. Both beliefs were 100% right, for exactly as long as I held them. Here is how they broke, why belief has to come before learning, and why some of the things I am bad at, I am bad at on purpose.

The step before self-learning

Learn it

Frameworks expire, languages drift and certificates go stale. The ability to self-learn keeps paying out, and it starts with a step so early that most of us walk straight past it. And the half that motivational posters leave out: being able to learn something is not the same as having to learn it.

🔒 One Sentence, Many Costumes

As a mentor and as a manager, I have heard one sentence more than any other. It rarely shows up in its own clothes.

"I am not a numbers person."
"I am just not technical."
"I am not a natural leader."
"Some people just get it, and I am not one of them."
▼   different words, same sentence underneath   ▼

"I was not born for this."

Step 1 🙅
You hold the belief

It feels like a plain fact, not a belief.

➤
Step 2 🚪
You never start

The editor stays closed. The lead role gets turned down.

➤
Step 3 ⚖️
Or you start, braced to fail

The first error reads like a verdict instead of a clue.

➤
Step 4 🧾
The "proof" arrives

No clumsy attempts collected, so no competence built.

↻   the belief now looks proven, and the loop starts again

The belief does not describe your ability. It manufactures the evidence that proves it right, for exactly as long as you believe it.

🐛 Nobody Was Born a Programmer

For the first generation it was literally impossible: when they were born, there was nothing to program yet. Everyone since has had to learn it, me included.

What I believed

Good programmers do not make bugs.

So someone who knew they would make bugs could never be one.

➤
What is actually true

Every developer makes bugs, including the very best.

Even AI makes them, which makes sense when you remember who it learned to code from: us.

The skill was never "do not make bugs". The skill is catching them.

🧪 Testing 👀 Reviewing 🔍 Debugging 🧱 Habits that make carelessness manageable

Every one of those is learned. Nobody is born knowing how to read a stack trace.

AI changed what you have to learn. It did not remove the need to learn it.

⚔️ 100% Right, or 50% Right

A saying I have carried for years, more honest than most motivational quotes. In learning, the battle is the evening you decide whether to open the tutorial, or the moment after your third failed attempt.

Go in believing you will lose
100% right, every time
LossGuaranteed

Disbelief guarantees the loss. With a guaranteed loss, you have no chance at all.

Go in believing you will win
50% right, maybe
WinLose

Belief merely keeps the outcome open. With 50%, you have a chance.

Possunt, quia posse videntur.

"They can, because they think they can."

Virgil, Aeneid, Book V

The ending nobody quotes

Two thousand years ago, Virgil gave that line to the crew chasing the leader in a boat race. What gets quoted far less often is how the race ends: they drew almost level, and still finished second. Virgil understood the 50% too.

Belief does not win the battle. It gets you onto the field.

🌉 How the Belief Finally Broke

Not through inspiration, and not through confidence. One belief broke because a job pushed me. For the other one, I jumped.

🧭 Leadership

Pushed
  1. 📘A book planted the doubt. It could not make me a leader, but it made me suspicious of the story I had been telling myself.
  2. 🚢A job left no opting out. As an import and export manager reaching customers overseas, I had people to lead, born for it or not.
  3. 📈Practice did the real work. Every day, with real stakes and real feedback. I was not half as bad as I had thought.

💻 Programming

Jumped
  1. ✨Curiosity lit the spark. When our company set out to build its first website, I took an HTML and CSS course on a free LinkedIn Learning trial.
  2. 🤝A leap of faith. A friend, who became one of my early mentors, convinced me to quit import and export and move into tech.
  3. 🐛Bugs, exactly as predicted. Plenty of them. But by then I knew everybody makes them. They were simply part of the job.
Neither move proved I was secretly born for it. You do not need to be born with it, because you can learn it.

⏳ Anything, but Not Everything

"You can learn anything!" True, as far as it goes. But being able to learn anything does not mean you can learn everything, and it certainly does not mean you should.

Every skill has a price
💵 Money: courses, books, equipment ⏳ Time: practice, and months of being bad 🔐 Gates: licences, supervised training
≈ 4,000
weeks, if you live to eighty

Time is the currency that matters most, because it is the one you can never earn back. Every skill is paid for out of this budget, and every yes to one skill is a quiet no to another.

From Oliver Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks

So some things I choose to be bad at

🏸

A bad badminton player

I am quite sure I could learn it, given the hours. For the life I actually live, it would give back very little, and I am at peace with that.

Chose not to
🚗

Cannot drive a car

Not because I am incapable. A taxi takes me anywhere I need to go, and learning it, then keeping it sharp, would cost real time for little return.

Chose not to
Choosing what to be bad at is not a failure of ambition. It is what makes being good at anything possible.

🔑 Cannot, or Chose Not To?

From the outside, the two look identical. From the inside, they could not be more different.

I can't
A belief about my limits.Closes a door I never actually tried.
I chose
not to
A decision about my priorities.Walks past a door I know I could open.

Next time you catch yourself thinking you are bad at something, ask yourself: Do you believe you cannot learn it, or did you choose not to?

✅ If you chose not to

Good. Own it. Put it on your list, right next to mine.

🏸 My badminton 🚗 My driving ➕ Yours goes here

Do not waste a minute feeling guilty. That is not a weakness. It is a strategy.

🔍 If you believe you cannot

Look hard at where that belief came from.

A test you failed once A teacher's offhand comment A film where the hero was born brilliant

You only have to stop being certain that you cannot. That moves you from 100% to 50%, and 50% is a chance.

🧪

Then make it a real choice

Give it a small, honest test: a few weeks, one small project. Keep going if it is worth your time, or move it, honestly this time, into "chose not to".

Both are fine. The only answer that is not fine is the one you never actually chose.

You were probably not born for it. Almost nobody was.
You can still learn it, if you decide it is worth your time.

Self-learning starts one step earlier than most people think: not with a course or a plan, but with a quiet decision to stop believing the sentence that has been keeping you out. So, what is on your list? Which of the things you are bad at did you choose, and which ones did you only ever believe?
